Geopolitical Risk across Asset Classes

Master Thesis

Read abstract

I test whether geopolitical risk is a priced state variable. In an intertemporal capital asset pricing model with the log geopolitical risk (GPR) index of Caldara and Iacoviello (2022) as the state variable, assets that appreciate when geopolitical tension rises are hedges and should earn lower expected returns. Across individual stocks in 33 markets, the 49 U.S. industry portfolios and 36 national equity indices, frequentist and Bayesian estimates of the geopolitical risk premium and of its market price of risk are centered on zero. Measurement accounts for much of this null: among individual stocks, the spread in formation-period geopolitical betas vanishes out of sample, and aggregation into industries and indices recovers only part of it. Geopolitical news nonetheless moves prices on impact. It raises the option-implied probability of a market crash, largely in common with implied volatility, lowers short-term Treasury yields and loads with opposite signs on the local-equity and currency components of international returns, but these responses are small, short-lived, or both. Geopolitical risk thus appears to be a source of transitory repricing rather than a persistently priced factor.

The Price of Sustainability: Revealed ESG Preferences

Undergraduate Thesis

Read abstract

I use the algebra of the ESG-efficient frontier to construct an index of revealed ESG taste, in units of forgone Sharpe ratio, from prices and scores alone. Estimated on ESG-sorted portfolios of the S\&P~500, the STOXX Europe~600 and the Nikkei~225 over 2011--2024, the measure is indistinguishable from zero in the full sample and moves enough on rolling windows to change sign: it rises through 2021 in the United States and Europe and turns negative by 2024. Sorting within sector as well as within market preserves the European episode, concentrated in 2018--2021, and eliminates the American one, much of which reflects industry exposure. The rise and most of the decline are repricings of the characteristic, whereas the final change of sign reflects the drift of the market portfolio along the frontier. The index co-moves with flows into sustainable funds, and institutional ESG demand estimated from holdings predicts subsequent portfolio scores and Sharpe-ratio sacrifices, although holdings adjust more slowly than prices.
